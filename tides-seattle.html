The pattern

Seattle commonly sees tidal ranges pushing past 10 feet on the bigger exchanges of the month, with the mixed pattern making one high tide tower over the other. The extremes arrive around full and new moons. Low tides — especially the year’s lowest “minus tides” in late spring and summer — expose vast stretches of beach.

Boating in the Sound

Elliott Bay boaters plan around the current more than the height: the Sound’s constrictions accelerate tidal flow, and an ebb against a northerly can make for a lumpy ride. Check the tide before threading the Ballard Locks or crossing to Bainbridge. Trailer boaters should note that some ramps go skinny at the lowest tides.

Fishing and foraging

Puget Sound salmon — Chinook and coho on their seasonal runs — respond to moving water, and many local anglers swear by the tide change. Minus tides are prime for beachcombing and clamming. For the broader region, see our Pacific Northwest tide guide.
