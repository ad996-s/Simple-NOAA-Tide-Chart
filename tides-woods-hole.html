Woods Hole Tide Chart

Woods Hole — Cape Cod’s ocean-science village — sees a modest two-foot semidiurnal tide, but the Hole’s narrow passage turns that small range into some of New England’s most famous currents.

The pattern

Two highs and two lows daily with around two feet of range — small water with outsized consequences. The tide rips through Woods Hole passage at several knots, and the timing rules everything from ferries to fishing.

Science-town stripers

Striped bass stack in the Hole’s rips and along the Elizabeth Islands on moving water; the institution’s docks watch it all. Black sea bass and scup fill the bay side.

Boating

Time the passage — the current runs hard enough to stop a small boat, and the ferries to Martha’s Vineyard thread it daily. Slack water is the kind window. For the region, see our New England tide guide.
