Plan Your Fishing Trip Around the Tides

Updated October 18, 2026 · Simple Tides guides

Ask any saltwater guide the single biggest factor in a good day, and most will say the tide — not the lure, not the spot. Moving water moves bait, and bait moves predators. This guide teaches you to plan trips around the tide and match your gear to each stage.

Why tides matter more than lures

Tides create current. Current pushes baitfish and shrimp off the flats and out of the marsh; predator fish set up on points, cuts and creek mouths to ambush them. Slack water — the pause between tides — usually means the bite pauses too.

Fish each tide stage

  • Rising (incoming): fish push onto flats and into the marsh. Topwater plugs and live shrimp under corks shine. Wade or kayak shallow.
  • High slack: tough window — fish scatter. Downsize lures, fish deeper holes and channels.
  • Falling (outgoing): the prime bite. Bait drains off the flats; set up at creek mouths and cuts with paddletails and live bait.
  • Low slack: fish stack in holes and channels. Slow down with jigs and Carolina rigs.

Match gear to the tide

  • Strong current: heavier jigheads (3/8–1/2 oz), braided line for sensitivity, anchor or stake-out pole for kayaks.
  • Slack water: light jigheads (1/8 oz), fluorocarbon leaders, finesse presentations.
  • Night tides: dock lights + free-lined shrimp — bring a headlamp with red mode.

Frequently asked questions

What tide is best for saltwater fishing?

Moving tides — the two hours on either side of high or low — beat slack water almost every time. Falling tides get the nod as the single most productive stage.

Do fish bite during slack tide?

Less. Without current, bait scatters and predators get lazy. Fish deeper structure slowly, or use the slack window to relocate to your next falling-tide spot.

How do moon phases affect fishing?

New and full moons create the biggest tidal swings (spring tides) and strongest currents — usually the best fishing of the month. Quarter moons mean weak neap tides and slower days.

What gear do I need for tide-based fishing?

Versatility: jigheads from 1/8 to 1/2 oz to match current speed, braided line for feel, and both topwater and subsurface lures to cover rising and falling stages.
