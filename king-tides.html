King Tides 2026–2027: Dates & Viewing Guide

The next king tide window opens October 25–30, 2026 on the Southeast coast — the highest tides of the year, predictable weeks in advance. Here are the 2026–2027 dates for every US coast, the best places to watch, and how to stay safe.

What a king tide is

A king tide is an especially large spring tide: the moon at its closest approach to Earth (perigee) lines up with a new or full moon, and the combined pull stacks the tide higher than usual — a few times a year at any given spot. They’re the tides that flood low-lying streets on otherwise sunny days. For the full explainer, see what a king tide is.

Best places to watch

Rocky headlands and elevated viewpoints beat low beaches — you see the scale without standing in it. Good bets on our tide charts:

  • Pacific Northwest: Newport, OR, Neah Bay, WA, Friday Harbor, WA
  • California: Long Beach, San Diego
  • Southeast: Hilton Head, SC, Nags Head (Outer Banks), NC, Savannah, GA, Nantucket, MA, Montauk, NY, Bar Harbor, ME
  • Gulf: Destin, FL, Panama City Beach, FL, Port Aransas, TX, South Padre Island, TX

Safety: read this before you go

King tides are predictable, but the ocean around them isn’t. On the West Coast they arrive with winter surf, and sneaker waves can run far up the beach without warning.

  • Watch from high ground — headlands, overlooks, upper beach stairs.
  • Stay off jetties, rock outcroppings, and low beaches at the peak of the tide.
  • Never turn your back on the ocean.
  • Keep kids and pets well back from the waterline.
  • In towns: don’t drive through saltwater-flooded streets — it wrecks cars.
