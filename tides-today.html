Today’s high and low tides

Every coast runs its own schedule today. Most US coasts see two high tides and two low tides daily (semidiurnal); the Gulf Coast mostly sees one of each (diurnal). Times shift about 50 minutes later each day, so today’s tides won’t match yesterday’s. Each city page below shows today’s exact highs and lows from live NOAA predictions — no signup, no clutter.

How to read today’s tide chart

On any city’s tide chart, the peaks are high tides and the valleys are low tides, with heights in feet above MLLW (mean lower low water). The steepest parts of the curve are when water moves fastest — that’s when fishing, boating, and beach plans feel the tide most. For the full walkthrough, see our guide to reading tide charts.

Tides today FAQs

What time is high tide today?

High tide times are different for every coastal location and shift about 50 minutes later each day. Pick your city from the list below or open the tide map to see today's exact high tide times from live NOAA predictions.

What time is low tide today?

Low tide today depends on where you are — times and heights vary by location and move later each day. Every city tide chart on Simple Tides shows today's live NOAA low tides.

How do I read today's tide chart?

Find today's date on the chart: the peaks are high tides, the valleys are low tides, and the height numbers tell you how high or low the water gets. Our tide chart guide walks through it step by step.

Are today's tides the same as yesterday's?

Almost, but about 50 minutes later. The moon's orbit shifts each day's tides later, and the heights change with the moon phase — see spring vs neap tides.
