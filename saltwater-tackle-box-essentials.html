Saltwater Tackle Box Essentials Checklist

Updated October 18, 2026 · Simple Tides guides

A good saltwater tackle box isn't the biggest one — it's the one with the right things. This checklist covers the lures, terminal tackle and tools that handle 90% of coastal fishing, whether you're on a pier, in the surf, or in a kayak.

Lures that earn their space

  • Soft plastic paddletails (3–5"): the most versatile saltwater lure ever made — jigheads from 1/8 to 1/2 oz.
  • Spoons (1/2–1 oz gold/silver): cast far, catch everything, nearly indestructible.
  • Topwater plug: dawn patrol redfish and trout — the most fun bite in fishing.
  • Shrimp imitation: deadly under a popping cork for trout and reds.
  • Bucktail jigs: for deeper water, jetties and bridge pilings.

Terminal tackle

  • Circle hooks: 1/0–5/0 assortment
  • Jigheads: 1/8, 1/4, 3/8, 1/2 oz
  • Swivels and snaps (black, corrosion-resistant)
  • Bullet and egg sinkers: 1/4–2 oz
  • Pyramid sinkers: 2–4 oz (surf)
  • Fluorocarbon leader: 20, 30, 50 lb spools
  • Popping corks

Tools and spares

  • Corrosion-resistant pliers with cutters
  • Dehooker
  • Knife
  • Tape measure / bump board (know your slot limits)
  • Spare braid and leader
  • Sun gloves and lip gripper
  • Small first-aid kit

Organize by trip type

Keep a small 'go box' with one tray of inshore essentials so you're not hauling the whole arsenal for a two-hour trip. Restock it after every outing — the fish you miss is always on the lure you left at home.

Frequently asked questions

What lures do I need for saltwater fishing?

Start with soft plastic paddletails on jigheads, a gold spoon, and a topwater plug. Those three cover the vast majority of inshore situations for trout, redfish and snook.

What size hooks for saltwater?

Circle hooks from 1/0 to 5/0 cover most coastal fishing: 1/0–2/0 for trout and whiting, 3/0–5/0 for redfish, snook and surf species.

How do I keep tackle from rusting?

Rinse boxes after salt trips, dry everything before closing lids, toss in silica gel packs, and spray metal tools with corrosion inhibitor. Stainless and coated hooks last far longer.

Mono or fluorocarbon leader for saltwater?

Fluorocarbon for leaders — it's nearly invisible underwater and far more abrasion-resistant than mono, which matters around shells, rocks and toothy fish.
